# Viking Raiders Returns — Working Instructions

## Project vision

Build a light-hearted, turn-based strategy game inspired by the simplicity and spirit of the ZX Spectrum game *Viking Raiders*. It is not intended to be a faithful copy. Preserve the approachable core idea while developing original presentation, rules, content, code, graphics, and sound.

The game owner is not a programmer. Explain decisions in plain English, avoid unexplained jargon, and provide clear instructions whenever the owner needs to run or review something.

## Core design pillars

1. **Simple to learn:** Moving, buying units, attacking, and capturing a castle must be easy to understand.
2. **Quick and playful:** Target a complete match length of approximately 20–30 minutes.
3. **Casual rather than serious:** Prefer charm, colour, humour, and readable outcomes over simulation or dense strategy.
4. **Meaningful turns:** Keep choices interesting without giving players long lists of actions or statistics.
5. **Fair uncertainty:** Random events may create surprises and catch-up opportunities, but should not routinely erase good play.
6. **Original identity:** Use the source game as historical inspiration, not as material to reproduce verbatim.

## Confirmed game requirements

- One to four players.
- Every player slot can be controlled by a human or the computer.
- Initial human multiplayer is shared-device hot-seat play.
- Keep a future route open for online multiplayer, but do not make it an initial requirement.
- Turn-based play.
- Every turn has two committed phases. **Viking Manoeuvres** comes first and permits movement, direct combat, boarding, disembarking, and Catapult fire in any unit order; purchasing is locked. The player presses **Viking Manoeuvres Complete** to commit and enter **Loot Spending**, after which no unit action may be taken and legal units may be purchased beside home. **Loot Spending Complete** resolves the Saga Card when enabled and advances to the next player. If no unit has a legal move, say so clearly without completing the phase automatically. A Catapult that moves must immediately fire or explicitly skip its shot before another unit can act or manoeuvres can be completed.
- The setup screen includes **Random starting player** and **Random player order** toggles, both enabled by default. Random starting player chooses which participating faction begins. Random player order seedably shuffles the complete turn sequence once at setup; that sequence then remains fixed for the entire match. Disabling both preserves the faction order shown on the setup screen. The chosen first player and complete order are announced in the game log.
- The main objective is to attack and capture opposing castles.
- Movement is simple and occurs in straight lines. Soldiers may voluntarily move 0–9 spaces, or make a forced run past nine spaces only to the first blocking object, board edge, or incompatible terrain; clear intermediate squares at 10+ spaces are not legal voluntary destinations. Soldiers cannot occupy water except while embarked in a Longboat. Longboats and Catapults move up to nine spaces.
- Board obstacles stop movement rather than merely preventing occupation of their own square. Drinking Horns stop every unit's travel; a Soldier may enter the horn square but must end movement there and become drunk, while Catapults cannot enter or pass it. Castles remain physical blocking structures after their faction is defeated. Future saga-card obstacles must be added through the shared obstacle check so Soldiers and Catapults cannot bypass them.
- The board is viewed from above.
- The new battlefield should be materially larger than the original game's board while remaining readable without excessive scrolling.
- Before a match containing at least one human begins, show its generated battlefield as a read-only preview with **Redraw map** and **Keep fjord & sound the horn**. Every redraw creates a fresh seeded battlefield and starting arrangement; no actions or computer turns occur until the human accepts it. A computer-only match begins immediately without the preview.
- The Saga deck is expanded from twenty-nine to thirty-two physical cards by adding one **Yule Festival** and two copies of **Berserker Rage**. Yule Festival makes one randomly selected, sober, unembarked Soldier or Veteran from every surviving faction drunk; affected treasure is dropped normally, factions without an eligible Viking are skipped, and all affected units are shown. Berserker Rage marks one random friendly, unembarked Soldier or Veteran. The marked unit automatically wins its next direct clash with a Soldier or Veteran, whether attacking or defending, and then loses Rage. Rage provides no castle bonus and is not consumed by a castle attack. If both combatants have Rage, neither can claim the automatic advantage: resolve their normal rank odds and consume both. The status must remain visually distinctive on land and aboard a Longboat, and the Saga resolution must highlight every affected Viking.
- The Saga deck is expanded again from thirty-two to thirty-four cards with two benign cards: **The Tactical Goat** and **Helmet Inspection**. They display their illustrated card panel and humorous outcome but alter no game state. Benign cards and target-dependent cards without a legal target must not place a generic **Nothing happens** or **Nothing changes** notification over the battlefield; the card panel's outcome is sufficient.
- The Saga deck grows from thirty-four to thirty-five cards with **High Winds**. It chooses one random friendly, unfrozen Longboat that has a safe route and visibly blows the vessel, all embarked crew, and their carried treasure one to three squares in a random straight-line direction. Every intermediate and destination hull position must be open water; the drift cannot ram another vessel or cross land, ice, castles, units, or obstacles. If no friendly boat can drift safely, the card panel explains why and the battlefield remains unchanged.
- **Saga Deck Version 1** contains exactly forty physical cards in every match: thirty consequence cards and ten benign cards. The benign cards are seedably sampled from a pool of thirty-two comic moments at match creation; only those ten selected cards are in that match and they recur normally when its discard pile is reshuffled. Shield Wall visibly marks one friendly, unembarked Soldier or Veteran; that unit survives and consumes the protection on its next Catapult impact, but receives no benefit in direct combat or against castles. Favourable Tide visibly carries one friendly unfrozen Longboat, its crew, and carried treasure one to three safe open-water squares closer to the nearest surviving enemy castle; it cannot ram or cross obstructions. Forgotten Rations costs up to 2 available gold.
- Once the battlefield preview is accepted—or immediately in a computer-only match—the preview strip is replaced by a persistent Saga table. It shows a face-down draw pile on the left, the current-card position in the centre, and the discard pile on the right. Pile thickness and qualitative wording indicate their relative contents without displaying card counts. A drawn card visibly travels from the left pile to the reading panel, then its face travels to the right pile before its board effect resolves. When the deck empties, visible cards travel from right to left in a reshuffle animation before the next draw.
- Factions are distinguished by strong primary colours and clear iconography.
- Visual direction: polished retro pixel art with modest character and modern clarity.
- Units travel visibly between their starting and destination squares. Use brief type-specific placeholder motion cues (Soldier dust, Longboat wake, Catapult wheel marks) and anchored arrival feedback for combat, treasure, boarding, and castle capture; keep this presentation separate from core rules and the headless benchmark.
- Core unit types include Viking Soldiers, three-square horizontal Longboats, and two-square horizontal Catapults.
- A simple economy and unit-purchasing mechanic remains central.
- Each faction begins with 50 gold and gains 1 gold at the start of each later turn. Soldiers cost 2 gold, Longboats cost 4 gold, and Catapults cost 8 gold.
- Purchased units must be placed in a legal square directly adjacent to their owner's home castle. If no adjacent legal square exists, that unit cannot be bought.
- Each participating player adds one neutral treasure chest to a random land square at setup. Chests contain 10, 20, 30, 40, or 50 gold. Only a Soldier can collect a chest; collection uses that turn's move, then on a later turn the same Soldier ends movement adjacent to their own castle to bank it while remaining on that square. Any game effect that directly places a carrier beside home also banks the chest immediately, preventing a blocked courtyard.
- Each participating player adds two neutral Drinking Horns to random land squares. A Soldier who steps on one becomes permanently drunk, drops any carried treasure, cannot carry treasure or board a Longboat, and makes an uncontrolled computer-directed opening move at the start of each turn. That move chooses a random direction and either a random 1–9-square legal move (75%) or the furthest legal run in that direction (25%). Computer Soldiers not carrying treasure have a small preference for stepping onto a horn. Drunk Soldiers can still attack Catapults and castles; when another faction attacks a drunk Soldier, the defender changes to the attacker’s faction, sobers up, and remains in place while the attacker’s action is spent. The conversion presentation keeps both pieces on their true squares, retains the defender's former colour and drunk icon until the action completes, and reveals the new sober faction only with the result.
- Square tiles versus hexagonal tiles is intentionally undecided and should be prototyped before committing.
- About two-thirds of maps use one connected fjord. About one-third may use a deliberately classic-style central land mass with two or more separate waterways; these must read as intentional navigable fjords rather than accidental lakes.
- Fjord channels must be broad enough for three-square Longboats. Before seasonal freezing, every water component must be usable by a boat; decorative one-square water notches are not allowed.
- Fjord generation may be asymmetric on an individual map, but its fixed recipe must not systematically favour the left or right half of the board across seeded matches. Water remains two to four squares from the left and right board edges, leaving usable land banks and never a one-square strip.
- Longboats always occupy three horizontal squares, regardless of their movement direction, and carry up to three Viking Soldiers. A neighbouring Soldier may embark by moving onto a friendly boat only; enemy Longboats are impassable and cannot be boarded or attacked by Soldiers. Embarked Soldiers may later disembark onto an open adjacent land or ice square. A human selects the exact passenger to disembark; the crew list identifies Soldiers, Veterans, and treasure carriers. A Longboat carrying any treasure displays a gold-chest marker on its hull. Treasure remains attached to its carrier throughout ordinary boarding, travel, and disembarking, but sinks if the boat and carrier are destroyed.
- Every participating castle must share a connected landmass with at least one opponent's castle. Prefer placing all castles on the main landmass so land warfare is always viable.
- Every home castle must have a directly adjacent centre square for a usable three-square horizontal Longboat berth. Each faction's starting force is one Longboat and three Soldiers; Catapults must be purchased. All starting units, including the initial Longboat's centre square, must be directly adjacent to their owner’s castle. A player may need to move that starting boat before buying another if it occupies the only berth.
- Castle placement uses the four intentionally asymmetric home regions with limited controlled variation, but seeded randomness assigns faction colours to those regions afresh for every match. No colour owns a permanent corner. Placement rejects any castle pairing that gives a legal starting courtyard square an unobstructed straight Soldier attack on another castle; fjord geometry must force a more interesting approach rather than allowing a first-turn forced run into a castle.
- Two-player matches select diagonally opposite home regions and never adjacent corners. Three-player matches use a broad triangular layout rather than an L-shaped selection of three corners: one castle begins near the centre of a long board edge and two are spread across the opposite edge, with seeded inversion and faction assignment. Preserve every existing land-route, mooring, starting-formation, asymmetry, and first-turn attack rejection rule.
- Computer players use persistent, serializable faction plans and unit roles rather than arbitrary movement. Each faction selects an objective—defend its castle, deliver or recover treasure, capture an immediately reachable castle, attack a chosen rival castle, or consolidate—and retains ordinary campaign targets according to difficulty. Units are assigned as carriers, hunters, defenders, raiders, transports, naval interceptors, or siege support. Target-castle selection weighs route length, defenders, committed friendly forces, and enemy army size. Distant enemies are monitored, but only Soldiers within roughly six terrain-route steps trigger a defensive recall. Defender numbers cannot exceed the approaching threat count, and defenders prefer a loose interception ring two to four squares from home; they occupy the courtyard only when an attacker is at the gate. Units may deliberately hold position when no move improves their role, especially Catapults and castle defenders. Longboat transport uses persistent, serializable missions: the computer compares the complete pickup, sailing, landing, and final approach against the land route; assigns one to three named raiders according to difficulty; holds the boat at pickup until they board; and keeps them aboard until a useful shore lies within eight spaces of the chosen castle. Expert launches with two crew after one full waiting round rather than indefinitely waiting for a third. Dead, drunk, treasure-carrying, or reassigned crew are immediately removed and replaced while loading. Winter-invalidated routes are cancelled. A landing completes the mission and gives that boat a two-round cooldown, preventing the landing party from being ordered straight back aboard. A second Expert mission may begin only after the first boat has actually departed. Empty boats without a worthwhile transport mission only pursue an enemy boat within immediate movement range, otherwise holding position. A second Longboat becomes a purchase candidate only after an existing transport has crew aboard and enough uncommitted raiders remain. Immediate castle-attack likelihood is 65% on Very Easy, 80% on Easy, 90% on Normal, and 100% on Hard and Expert. Every treasure carrier is assessed independently, an available deposit outranks all other AI actions, and a fully blocked courtyard is cleared once any carrier is estimated within two turns. Soldiers already beside their home castle bank carried treasure without moving away and returning. Computer purchases are scored from army composition, real defensive pressure, unclaimed treasure, nearby enemy boats, and proven transport demand. An unpressured faction normally stops at twelve units, but may exceed that soft limit when outnumbered or defending. All five difficulties receive identical rules, resources, and two computer purchase opportunities per turn; strength comes from routing quality, plan persistence, threat horizon, role coordination, move and purchase standards, and deliberate variation.
- Combat judgement and coordinated attacks scale explicitly with difficulty while rules and probabilities remain identical. Very Easy largely ignores combat odds and attacks alone; Easy applies only light judgement. Normal begins weighing expected losses and assembling two-raider pushes. Hard evaluates combat value, local numerical superiority, nearby friendly and enemy support, protects Veterans more carefully, coordinates two-raider attacks, and moves the committed pair before unrelated units. For ordinary choices where at least two useful actions or purchases exist, Hard deliberately accepts the second-ranked option 20% of the time; it never applies this variation to an available castle attack or mandatory treasure deposit. Expert consistently chooses the highest-scored option, applies the strongest risk assessment while exploiting local superiority, reduces its Veteran caution when that Veteran has meaningful support, gives high priority to defenders blocking the chosen castle, favours exposed castles, and moves both committed raiders before a third reinforcement. Stronger raiders may hold five to eight squares from their chosen castle while partners catch up, but any Soldier already able to attack the castle retains the established high attack priority. Expert builds toward eight Soldiers before considering a second Longboat and keeps the smallest idle gold reserve. Expert also applies the strongest penalty to idle units crowding an unthreatened home castle, favouring Catapults against substantial land forces and Longboats only when they have a real transport or interception purpose. A Soldier attacking a Catapult recognises the guaranteed victory as a decisive opportunity.
- A Catapult may move up to nine spaces and fire once per turn at an intended square up to eleven spaces away. If it moves before firing, it must take its firing opportunity immediately; beginning another action or declining the shot forfeits firing for that round. A stationary Catapult may fire, but firing commits it to remain stationary for the rest of that round. Its projectile arcs over intervening objects and lands within a visible scatter radius; the actual impact square determines what is hit. A player cannot deliberately select a friendly unit as the intended target, although scatter may still cause accidental friendly fire. At the five-by-five long-range scatter area, each outer-ring square is half as likely as an inner three-by-three square. Normal computer Catapults consider target value and basic risk. Hard and Expert Catapults use defence, counter-battery, naval-interdiction, and siege priorities; evaluate the weighted scatter area; prefer useful six-to-nine-square firing positions with a nearby escort; avoid friendly-fire risk; reassess after each shot; and fire before a coordinated assault when already in position. Expert applies the strongest expected-value and friendly-safety judgement.
- Catapult presentation shows a stone arcing from the weapon to the actual scattered landing square, followed by a brief flash, debris, smoke, a temporary fading crater, and an anchored result. A destroyed Catapult remains briefly as a visibly broken, collapsed weapon with scattered parts before it fades away, whether it is defeated directly or by a projectile. The temporary scatter-area preview is sufficient aiming feedback: do not show a yellow intended-square circle or a permanent black impact X. A water landing uses a splash rather than a crater, and a hit result is reported only when a unit was actually struck; computer shots follow the selected animation speed.
- Rules resolve before animations, but a unit removed by an attack must be retained only in the presentation snapshot until the projectile or attacker visibly reaches it. It disappears, sinks, or becomes wreckage on the contact frame—not before. The removed unit must never remain in serializable game state, AI targeting, subsequent shot contexts, or later impact/sound results.
- Catapult fire has original procedural release and flight-whistle sounds synchronized to its animation. Water impacts use the supplied splash sample only when they land on water, while a Longboat sunk by either a Catapult or a ram uses the supplied bubbles beneath an original hull-break layer. A destroyed Catapult uses the supplied wooden-collapse sound whether it is struck directly or by a projectile; a Catapult projectile that removes a Soldier or Veteran uses the supplied comic impact cry. Sound effects are enabled by default but can be disabled on the setup screen. Never use audio as the only indication of an outcome.
- A direct Soldier-or-Veteran clash is shown briefly at the contact square with both combatants, crossed weapons, and sparks before its existing Victory or Repelled result. The attacking side receives a short procedural cheer on victory; a failed attacker receives a short procedural groan. A castle assault plays the opening three seconds of the supplied Viking celebration, fading through its final 700 ms, during the three-second suspense animation; it plays whether the result is Victory or Repelled and ends before the successful-capture horn. These are presentation-only effects and must never affect seeded rules or benchmark outcomes.
- Treasure banking has a short coin chime, drinking from a horn has a comic hiccup, and sobering—whether from a card, persuasion, or combat conversion—has a brief relieved exhale. These sounds are optional setup-controlled feedback and never replace visible outcomes.
- Starting a match plays the first four seconds of the supplied cinematic Viking battle-horn effect, fading continuously to silence through its final 850 ms. The battlefield is concealed behind a brief horn ceremony until the call has fully ended; the ceremony then fades over one further second to reveal the battlefield. No additional chief-specific horn plays at the end of a match. Sound effects respect the setup toggle and do not affect game rules.
- Sound effects and music have separate setup toggles. The supplied **Raise the Horn and Raid** music track loops quietly only while the war-council faction-selection interface is visible. It starts after an eligible user interaction, stops and rewinds before the battlefield preview or match appears, and resumes on returning to setup. The supplied licence-free Viking music track loops quietly when the Battle Report is shown and continues while the final battlefield is inspected; it stops only on leaving the completed match. Both music loops follow the Music toggle, while horns and gameplay effects follow the Sound effects toggle.
- Every castle attack is one continuous fixed three-second suspense sequence: the attacker travels once from its true starting square to the last clear square beside the castle, attacks from there, and never enters the castle square or restarts its charge. A successful long-distance attacker remains on that adjacent attack square; a failed attacker is removed there. During the sequence the walls shake and the result remains concealed. The castle and all surviving units that will change allegiance retain their original faction colours throughout both the approach and the complete suspense animation. Their captured colours, and the darkened castle, are revealed only with the Victory result. Capturing a castle then replays the full four-second cinematic battle horn used at match start, including its final fade, while a large on-board **Viking Victory!** banner celebrates the captured castle. No further faction-specific winner horn follows if the capture ends the match.
- When sound effects are enabled, the opening battle horn is a brief pre-turn ceremony: human input, purchases, drunk opening moves, and computer actions remain unavailable until its full call ends. The board explicitly says why play is waiting.
- When the final castle falls and all victory presentation has completed, show a **Battle Report**. It summarises rounds and turns, casualties, treasure, Saga Cards, Catapult activity, Longboat losses, each faction's final position, and humorous seeded-independent honours such as Sharpest Axe, Golden Helmet, Harbour Menace, and Mead Hall Frequent Visitor. Statistics are maintained as explicit serializable counters rather than inferred from player-visible log text. The report may be dismissed to review the final battlefield and reopened from the command panel.
- **Winter Is Coming** is an optional setup rule enabled by default. Its seeded start occurs after a randomly chosen completed round from 4 through 10. Water and neighbouring land whiten from north to south across six full-round phases; every surviving faction takes a turn before the next phase. Each newly affected band visibly fades from water and grass into ice and snow instead of changing instantly, accompanied by a clearly audible optional cold-wind gust while controls and computer actions wait. Water becomes ice that Soldiers and Catapults traverse as normal terrain. Longboats caught on ice remain visible but cannot move or accept new passengers. Their existing passengers automatically disembark onto available adjacent ice or land as the freeze reaches the boat; any passenger without a legal exit remains aboard until space opens. Show a brief board announcement when winter begins. No thaw is currently defined. Keep Winter disabled by default in the headless benchmark unless a winter balance run explicitly enables it.
- Combat has clear, casual asymmetry: a Soldier always defeats a Catapult in direct combat; a Catapult impact sinks a Longboat; and a Longboat that rams an enemy Longboat always wins.
- Veteran promotion is an experimental, deliberately reversible mechanic. A Soldier that survives a direct victory over a Soldier or Veteran becomes a Veteran; Veterans cannot be purchased. Soldier-versus-Soldier and Veteran-versus-Veteran combat is 50/50. A Veteran has a 55/45 advantage against an ordinary Soldier. An ordinary Soldier has a 40% chance to capture a castle, while a Veteran has a 45% chance; a failed castle attacker is removed. Successful attackers remain adjacent to the castle. Veterans use a distinct bare-chested, axe-bearing icon.
- The setup screen offers three match-wide Saga Card frequencies: **Off**, **Every turn**, or **Every two turns** (default). A round is complete after every surviving faction has taken one turn. Round 1 is always card-free. Every turn draws one card for each faction after its turn in rounds 2, 3, 4, 5, and so on. Every two turns draws one card for each faction after its turn in even-numbered rounds 2, 4, 6, and so on. A turn without a scheduled draw does not consume a card. The deck has twenty-nine physical cards: Royal Ransom, Treasure Hunter, Veteran’s Tale, Sobering Thought, Ale Delivery, Hidden Hoard, Army Upkeep, Harbour Dues, Catapult Repairs, The Persuader, Called Home, Raven's Counsel, The Jarl's Beard, A Perfectly Ordinary Rock, Longboat Laundry, The Mead Inspector, Axe-Shaped Cloud, Man Overboard, two copies of Bad Viking, Bandits!, Inside Job, Loose Change, The Deserter, New Recruit, Wrong Address, The Jarl's Feast, and two copies of Sturdy Longboat. Called Home uses a clear square beside the home castle; if every such square is blocked, it uses the nearest clear land or ice square instead. It never stacks a Viking on another unit or any occupied part of a Longboat or Catapult, and never removes or replaces an occupant. Sturdy Longboat gives its drawing faction one persistent protection charge, whether or not it currently owns a Longboat; the next friendly Longboat that would be destroyed by a Catapult hit or enemy ram survives and consumes one charge, with a visible **Sturdy Longboat!** result. Man Overboard removes one randomly chosen embarked crew member; Bad Viking demotes one randomly chosen friendly Veteran; Bandits! removes one random unattended chest; Inside Job transfers half the drawing faction's gold, rounded down, to one random surviving rival. Loose Change awards 3 gold; The Deserter removes a random ordinary, unembarked, non-carrier Soldier; New Recruit creates one free Soldier on a legal square beside home; Wrong Address relocates one random Drinking Horn to an open land square within four spaces; and The Jarl's Feast costs up to 3 gold. Target-dependent cards from these additions visibly resolve as “nothing happens” when they have no legal target rather than drawing a replacement. The six deliberately benign cards also visibly resolve with humorous text but make no game-state change. Army Upkeep costs up to 10 gold, Harbour Dues costs up to 5 gold when the player owns a Longboat, and Catapult Repairs costs up to 4 gold when the player owns a Catapult; no effect can create negative gold. Other cards with no legal board effect are skipped for the next eligible card; resolved cards are discarded and the discard pile is seedably shuffled back into play when the deck is empty. Each result is shown in a clear illustrated card panel before play continues.

## Technology direction

Use free and open-source tools wherever practical.

- **Language:** TypeScript, configured strictly enough to catch common mistakes.
- **Game framework:** Phaser 4 (the free, open-source edition—not the separate Phaser AE product).
- **Web tooling:** Vite.
- **Testing:** Vitest for game-rule tests; add browser-level testing only when it provides clear value.
- **Desktop path:** Tauri, after the web version is stable.
- **Mobile path:** Capacitor, after touch controls and responsive layout have been proven in the web version.

Keep core game rules independent from Phaser rendering and input code. The rules should operate on plain serializable game state and explicit player commands. This makes computer players, saved games, testing, replays, and possible future online multiplayer much easier to implement.

Do not add paid services, subscriptions, proprietary dependencies, or accounts without the owner's explicit agreement.

## Development approach

- Build in small, playable milestones.
- Establish rules with simple placeholder art before investing heavily in final assets.
- Prefer the smallest implementation that proves a design decision.
- Write automated tests for economy, movement, combat, turn order, victory, and event-card effects.
- Make randomness seedable so a game can be reproduced when diagnosing a problem.
- Keep player-visible text outside core logic so it can be edited and eventually translated.
- Treat keyboard, mouse, touch, screen size, colour contrast, and colour-blind accessibility as design concerns from the start.
- Do not rely on colour alone to identify factions; pair colours with emblems, shapes, or patterns.
- Keep computer-player behaviour understandable and fast. It should provide a fun casual opponent, not perfect play.
- Allow each computer-controlled player slot to select its own difficulty.
- Implement computer capabilities as explicit, testable settings. Prefer differences in planning, risk assessment, targeting, purchasing, and use of special rules over hidden economic or combat advantages.
- If optional AI handicaps or bonuses are ever added, show them clearly during setup rather than concealing them.
- Record unresolved design choices and the reasoning behind important decisions in `GAME_DESIGN.md`.
- Keep the headless computer-versus-computer runner working through `npm run benchmark`. The visible game and benchmark must share the same computer-turn orchestration. Benchmark matches use deterministic seeds, omit rendering and animation delays, default to cards off and a 60-round safety limit, and report difficulty results plus gameplay diagnostics. Do not treat a small sample as definitive balance evidence; use multiple seeds and colour-swapped pairings.

## Communication and handoff

When completing work:

- Start with what changed and what the owner can now see or do.
- Explain how to try it using short, numbered steps.
- Mention unresolved choices or risks without assuming programming knowledge.
- Avoid asking the owner to edit source code unless they specifically want to.
- When presenting alternatives, describe their visible effect on the game rather than only their technical differences.

## Source-material handling

Reference files may include emulator snapshots, cassette inlay information, screenshots, manuals, and gameplay footage. Store them under `references/` and document their origin when known. Do not distribute copyrighted reference files as part of a public game build.

Extract principles and observations from the references. Do not directly copy original code, text, maps, sprites, music, sound effects, branding, or packaging unless the owner has confirmed the necessary rights.
